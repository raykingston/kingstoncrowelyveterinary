Is it an emergency?

Signs your animal needs an emergency vet

If you're not sure, call us. It's always better to ring and be reassured than to wait.

  • Difficulty breathing, choking or blue-tinged gums
  • Road traffic accidents, falls, dog bites or other injuries, even if your pet seems fine
  • Heavy bleeding that won't stop
  • Collapse, seizures or sudden weakness
  • Swallowed poison: rat bait, slug pellets, antifreeze, medicines, chocolate, grapes or raisins
  • A swollen, tight belly or retching without bringing anything up, especially in large dogs
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, especially with blood
  • Straining to pass urine, particularly male cats
  • Difficult birth (whelping, kittening or calving) that isn't progressing
  • Severe pain, eye injuries or heatstroke

While you wait

What to do in an emergency

  1. Stay calm and keep yourself safe. Injured animals can bite or kick, even gentle ones.
  2. Phone us on 022 21258 and tell us what happened, your animal's age and weight if you know it, and any medicines they take.
  3. If they've eaten something harmful, bring the packet or label with you. Don't try to make them vomit unless a vet tells you to.
  4. Keep them warm and still. Use a blanket or towel to carry small animals, and a pet carrier for cats.
  5. Press firmly on any bleeding with a clean cloth while you travel.
